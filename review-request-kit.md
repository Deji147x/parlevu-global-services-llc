# Review request kit

Your Google Business Profile shows **no reviews**. A competitor checked
earlier in this work had 36. Review count and recency are direct local-ranking
factors, and they are the first trust signal a seller looks for before handing
over a house. Nothing else in this project moves local visibility as much, and
nothing else is as cheap.

Every past seller is a request you have not sent.

---

## The link to send

**Canonical (get this once, use it forever):** Google Business Profile →
**Ask for reviews** → copy the short link. It looks like
`https://g.page/r/XXXXXXXX/review` and opens the review box directly.

That button is the only place the canonical short link exists, so it has to
come from you.

**Verified fallback**, if you want something today — this resolves (HTTP 200,
checked):

```
https://www.google.com/maps/place/?cid=17812201313039090928
```

It opens your listing rather than the review box, so it costs the reader one
extra tap. Use the `g.page` link when you have it.

---

## When to ask

Ask once, at the moment the relief is real: **the day after closing**, when
the money has landed and the house is no longer their problem. Not at
contract, not at settlement while they are signing things, and not three weeks
later when the feeling has faded.

One ask. If they do not respond, let it go — a second chase costs more
goodwill than the review is worth.

---

## Templates

Each is short on purpose. The ask is the whole message; anything else buries
it. Replace `{{NAME}}`, `{{LINK}}` and `{{STREET}}`.

### Text message — the one that actually works

```
Hi {{NAME}}, it's Deji at Parlevu. Glad we got {{STREET}} closed for you.

If the process felt straightforward, would you leave us a quick Google
review? It genuinely helps other Baltimore homeowners decide whether to
call us.

{{LINK}}

Either way, thanks for trusting us with it.
```

### Email

```
Subject: One small favour, {{NAME}}?

Hi {{NAME}},

Now that {{STREET}} is closed and settled, I wanted to say thanks for
working with us.

Most people who call us are nervous. They have usually been told a cash
sale means getting taken advantage of, and they have no way to judge
whether we are different. A review from someone who has actually been
through it answers that better than anything we could write ourselves.

If you have two minutes:
{{LINK}}

Honest is more useful than glowing. If something could have gone better,
I would rather hear it.

Thanks again,
Deji
Parlevu Global Services LLC
(667) 646-8306
```

### In person, at or after closing

> "One last thing, and no pressure at all — if this went the way you hoped,
> a Google review would help the next person in your position. I'll text you
> the link. Takes a minute."

Say it out loud, then send the link while you are still standing there. An ask
made in person and followed by a text converts far better than a text alone.

### If they are hesitant

> "Totally fine. And if there's something we could have done better, tell me
> directly instead — that's more useful to me than a review anyway."

This is not a trick to extract a review. If someone is unhappy, you want to
know, and you do not want it on the profile. Mean it.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not offer anything in exchange.** Google prohibits incentivised
  reviews, and a profile caught doing it can lose its reviews or be
  suspended. You are already rebuilding trust on this listing; do not
  risk it.
- **Do not bulk-send to everyone at once.** Ten reviews appearing in one
  afternoon on a profile that has had none for years looks exactly like what
  Google filters for. Space them out.
- **Do not write them yourself or ask staff to.** Same risk, worse
  consequence.
- **Do not chase.** One ask.

---

## Reply to every review

Replying is a ranking signal and a visible one: a seller reading your profile
sees whether you engage. Reply within a couple of days, briefly, and by name.

For a negative review, reply once, factually, without arguing. Future readers
are the audience, not the reviewer.

---

## A realistic target

Ten reviews would put you in a different bracket entirely on local search, and
ten is reachable from past clients alone. Three would already beat zero, which
is the number currently telling every visitor that nobody has ever used you.

Track it: note the date you asked and whether it landed. That is the whole
system — there is nothing to automate here, and automating it is precisely
what Google penalises.
