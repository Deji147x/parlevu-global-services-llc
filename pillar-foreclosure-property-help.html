Maryland Foreclosure Timeline: Your Window to Act

Understanding Maryland's foreclosure process is the first step to taking control back.

Month 1: Notice of Default & Intent to Foreclose — You miss 120+ days of payments. Lender sends a notice and gives you 30 days to catch up.
Month 2: Missed Payment Window — You have until day 120 after the first missed payment to stop the foreclosure by paying the full arrears (missed payments, fees, penalties).
Month 3: Notice of Foreclosure Sale — Lender publishes the sale in the newspaper. Auction date is set for 45-60 days out.
Month 4-5: Auction Date — Your home is sold at public auction. If the sale price is less than what you owe, you may owe a deficiency (the bank can still come after you for the difference, in some cases).

The critical window: Months 1-3. If you can sell your home for cash and close before the auction date, you stop the foreclosure entirely. Your credit is damaged less (a short sale looks better than a foreclosure). You recover more equity. You have control.

Once the auction happens, you have lost almost all leverage. Don't wait that long.

What Happens if the Foreclosure Goes to Auction

Your home is sold to the highest bidder. Often, that's a hedge fund, an investor, or the bank itself (taking back what you owe). The winning bid is usually far below market value — $150,000 for a $250,000 home is not uncommon because investors want a deal.

You're evicted. You have 30-60 days to move after the sale closes. You lose your home.

Your credit is severely damaged. A foreclosure stays on your credit report for 7 years. Your score drops 100-200 points overnight. You can't get a mortgage, a car loan, or even a decent credit card for years.

You may owe a deficiency. If the home sells for $150,000 and you owe $200,000, you may owe the $50,000 difference. Maryland law allows deficiency judgments in some foreclosure cases. The bank can garnish your wages or take your assets to satisfy the judgment.

You keep nothing. Everything goes to the bank, the auction house, and the fees. You walk away with zero.
