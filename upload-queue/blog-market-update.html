How to Read the Baltimore Market Before You Sell

Market reports go stale the week they are published. The signals underneath them do not. If you know which few numbers to look up and what each one tells you, you can read the Baltimore market yourself - for your street, this month - instead of relying on a headline written for somebody else's neighbourhood.

Start local, not citywide

A citywide median blends Canton rowhomes with West Baltimore fixer-uppers and tells you almost nothing about your house. Look at sales within a few blocks, in a similar condition, from the last three to six months. That is the market a buyer will actually compare you against, and it is what an appraiser will use too.

The four signals worth checking

1. Days on market

How long comparable homes sit before going under contract. Short and falling means buyers are competing and you have leverage on price. Long and rising means buyers are choosing, and an overpriced listing will sit - which usually ends in a price cut that costs more than pricing right would have.

2. List-to-sale price ratio

What homes sold for compared with their asking price. At or above asking, buyers are paying up. Consistently below, the market is quietly negotiating prices down after the listing goes live. This one number tells you more about pricing power than any median.

3. Mortgage rates

Rates decide how many buyers can qualify at your price point, not whether demand exists. When rates rise, fewer buyers can finance the same house, and the ones who remain are more selective. That hits homes needing work hardest, because a financed buyer's lender often will not approve a property with serious defects at all.

4. Inventory

How many comparable homes are for sale against how quickly they sell. Low inventory props prices up because buyers have few alternatives. Rising inventory means your house is competing with more options, and condition and price start to matter a great deal more.

What the signals mean for your house

  • Fast market, good condition: list it. Buyers are competing, and a well-presented home will likely net the most on the open market.
  • Slow market, good condition: list, but price to the recent sales rather than the hope, and budget for carrying costs while you wait.
  • Any market, needs significant work: this is where financed buyers struggle most. Repairs, lender restrictions and inspection renegotiation all land on you, and a direct sale for cash often compares well once you net those out.
  • Timeline you do not control: foreclosure, probate, divorce or relocation. Market conditions matter less than certainty, and the speed of a sale can outweigh squeezing out the last of the price.

Where to look the numbers up

Recent comparable sales and days on market are available through listing sites and any local agent will pull them for free. Maryland REALTORS publishes monthly county and city statistics, and current mortgage rates are published weekly. Check them close to when you plan to sell - a number from last season can point you the wrong way.
