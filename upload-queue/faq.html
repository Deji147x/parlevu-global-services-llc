FSBO: For Sale By Owner

What does FSBO mean in real estate?

FSBO stands for For Sale By Owner. It means the property owner sells directly to a buyer without hiring a listing agent to represent the seller. The owner takes responsibility for important tasks such as pricing, marketing, buyer communication, negotiation, and coordinating the closing.

More on this

Can I sell my house myself without a real estate agent in Maryland?

Yes. Homeowners can generally sell their property without hiring a listing agent. However, you still need to manage the sale correctly, including applicable disclosures, contracts, title matters, and settlement requirements. Parlevu offers FSBO consultation to help you understand the process.

More on this

Is selling FSBO better than selling to a cash home buyer?

It depends on your goals. FSBO can give you more direct control over pricing and negotiations, but you must handle marketing and buyer coordination. A cash sale may offer greater convenience and a faster timeline, especially when the property needs repairs. Compare the likely net proceeds, costs, effort, and certainty of each option.

More on this

How do I price my home correctly when selling it myself?

Start by reviewing recently sold comparable properties in your neighborhood. Consider square footage, condition, property type, upgrades, and current competition. An online estimate alone may not reflect your home's actual market value. Parlevu's FSBO consultation can help you evaluate pricing considerations for the Baltimore and DMV markets.

Yes. You can explore a direct cash-purchase option without going through a traditional MLS listing process. If you prefer to sell independently, our FSBO consultation can help you consider pricing, marketing, and negotiation. MLS access and any associated services depend on the arrangement you choose.

Do I still have to disclose property defects when selling as-is in Maryland?

Yes, applicable disclosure obligations still matter. Maryland law generally requires covered residential sellers to provide the prescribed disclosure or disclaimer statement, and known latent defects that pose a direct threat to health or safety must be disclosed under the applicable rules. Exceptions exist, so verify which requirements apply to your transaction.
