FSBO Paperwork & Contracts: Every Form You Need in MD, VA & DC

Are you a homeowner in Baltimore or surrounding areas considering selling your property on your own? Maryland, Virginia, and DC foreclosures can be a complex process, but with the right paperwork and contracts, you can navigate the process with confidence. In this guide, we'll walk you through the essential forms and documents you'll need to sell your home as a seller by owner (FSBO).

What Do I Need to Know About FSBO Paperwork?

In Maryland, VA, and DC, the Seller's Disclosure and Disclaimer Statement is a crucial document that must be completed by sellers in all residential sales, including as-is sales. This statement is designed to inform buyers about known material defects or issues with the property, and it's essential to disclose any defects to avoid potential lawsuits or disputes. Even if you're selling your home as-is, you must still provide this statement to potential buyers.

The Maryland Real Estate Commission requires that sellers use a standardized disclosure form, which can be obtained from the Commission's website. This form will guide you through the process of identifying and disclosing known defects or issues with your property.

It's also essential to note that Maryland foreclosures are quasi-judicial, meaning they can move quickly from Notice of Intent to auction in as little as 90-120 days. However, as an FSBO seller, you can sell your home at any point before the auction, giving you more control over the process.

Who Draws Up the FSBO Contract?

When selling your home as a seller by owner, you'll need to create a contract that outlines the terms of the sale, including the price, closing date, and any contingencies. In Maryland, VA, and DC, you can use a standardized FSBO contract form, which can be downloaded from the National Association of Realtors website. Alternatively, you can hire an attorney to help you create a custom contract that meets your specific needs.

It's essential to include a clear and comprehensive contract that protects both you and the buyer. This will help prevent disputes and ensure a smooth closing process.

What's the Process for FSBO Deed Transfer?

When it's time to transfer ownership of your home, you'll need to complete a Deed Transfer form, which is usually provided by the seller's title company or attorney. This form will outline the transfer of ownership and any other necessary details.

In Maryland, VA, and DC, the Deed Transfer form must be notarized and recorded with the local government, which can take several weeks to complete. It's essential to plan ahead and allow time for the transfer process to occur before closing on the sale of your home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who holds escrow in an FSBO transaction?

In an FSBO transaction, the escrow is typically held by the seller, but it's recommended to use a title company or attorney to handle the escrow process. This will help ensure that all parties are protected and that the sale closes smoothly. In Maryland, VA, and DC, the escrow can also be held by a third-party escrow company, which can provide an added layer of security and protection for all parties involved.

What's the difference between an FSBO contract and a traditional real estate contract?

An FSBO contract is a simplified contract that outlines the terms of the sale, including the price, closing date, and any contingencies. In contrast, a traditional real estate contract is more comprehensive and includes additional provisions, such as inspections and financing contingencies. When selling your home as an FSBO, it's essential to use a standardized FSBO contract form or hire an attorney to create a custom contract that meets your specific needs.

Do I need to hire an attorney to sell my home as an FSBO?

While it's not required to hire an attorney to sell your home as an FSBO, it's highly recommended. An attorney can help you create a custom contract that meets your specific needs, review the contract for any potential issues, and provide guidance throughout the entire process. In Maryland, VA, and DC, an attorney can also help you navigate the complex foreclosure process and ensure that all parties are protected.
